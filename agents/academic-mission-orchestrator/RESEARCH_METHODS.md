# Research Methods Playbook

## Feasibility-first design

When the evidence base is immature, prefer a feasibility/pilot question before confirmatory efficacy.

Primary feasibility domains may include:

- recruitment;
- retention;
- adherence;
- intervention fidelity;
- completeness;
- acceptability;
- safety/adverse events;
- protocol deviations.

## Pilot sample logic

Do not pretend a pragmatic pilot sample is powered for efficacy.

Use the pilot to estimate:

- process rates;
- variance;
- effect-size uncertainty;
- recruitment capacity;
- measurement burden;
- parameters for a later definitive trial.

## Randomization/assessment

Prefer:

- computer-generated allocation;
- allocation concealment when possible;
- baseline assessment before reveal;
- blinded outcome assessors when feasible;
- cointervention tracking.

## Intervention reporting

Use a reproducibility framework such as TIDieR where relevant.

Specify:

- dose;
- duration;
- frequency;
- instructor qualifications;
- progression;
- adaptations;
- stop rules;
- safety exclusions;
- fidelity checklist;
- audit proportion.

## Measurement

A measure should be selected for:

- construct match;
- population evidence;
- age range;
- responsiveness;
- ceiling/floor risk;
- feasibility;
- licensing;
- assessor burden.

Optional instrumentation should not become a single point of failure for a master's thesis.

## Software-assisted measurement

A software pipeline is exploratory until reliability/validity are demonstrated.

Recommended validation sequence:

1. deterministic/synthetic tests;
2. inter-rater or repeated-measure reliability;
3. comparison with reference method;
4. absolute error;
5. agreement analysis;
6. sensitivity to change;
7. external validation.

## Statistics for small pilots

Prefer estimation over dichotomous significance claims.

Report:

- rates/proportions;
- confidence intervals;
- baseline-adjusted differences where appropriate;
- small-sample effect sizes such as Hedges g when applicable;
- missingness/protocol deviations;
- ITT where meaningful;
- per-protocol sensitivity analysis.
